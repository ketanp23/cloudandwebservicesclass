Reference

Reading primer

A five-minute refresher on the ideas you'll use. Skim it now; come back to any part during the rounds.

1 · The cloud, in one line

The cloud is renting computing power (servers, storage, databases) over the internet instead of buying and running the machines yourself. Like electricity: you plug in, use what you need, and get a monthly bill — no power plant of your own.

2 · The financial shift: CapEx → OpEx

Owning hardware is a large, up-front Capital Expenditure (CapEx) that depreciates and locks up cash. Cloud turns it into a flexible, monthly Operating Expenditure (OpEx) that rises and falls with actual use. Finance teams value this because spending flexes with revenue and capital isn't tied up in fast-obsoleting equipment.

3 · Service models — how much is done for you

  • IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service): you rent raw servers & storage and still run the software. Most control, most effort.
  • PaaS (Platform as a Service): the provider runs the servers and operating system; you just deploy your app.
  • SaaS (Software as a Service): ready-made software you simply log in to — Gmail, Zoom, Salesforce.

4 · The 6 R's — one strategy per system

You rarely move everything the same way. For each application, pick the R that best balances effort, cost and benefit:

StrategyNicknameIn plain terms
RehostLift & shiftMove it as-is to cloud servers. Fast, least benefit.
ReplatformLift & tweakSmall optimisations (e.g. a managed database) while moving.
RepurchaseDrop & shopReplace it with a SaaS product.
RefactorRe-architectRebuild it to be cloud-native. Most effort, most long-term gain.
RetireSwitch offTurn off apps nobody really uses — instant saving.
RetainRevisit laterLeave it where it is for now — not ready or not worth moving.

5 · Building the business case

  • TCO (Total Cost of Ownership): every cost over a period — hardware, data-centre, licences, staff, downtime, and on the cloud side, migration + monthly usage. Compare on-premise TCO vs cloud TCO.
  • ROI (Return on Investment): net benefit ÷ cost. Did the money returned beat the money spent?
  • Payback period: how long until the savings recover the migration spend.
  • Intangibles: benefits a spreadsheet misses — no crashes, faster launches, happier customers. Name them; quantify where you can.

6 · Design — the six pillars

A good cloud design answers six manager-level questions (the "Well-Architected" pillars):

  • Cost — are we spending efficiently, with no waste?
  • Reliability — will it stay up and recover from failures?
  • Performance — is it fast enough at peak scale?
  • Security — is data and access properly protected?
  • Operations — can we monitor, update and run it smoothly?
  • Sustainability — are we mindful of energy and efficiency?

7 · Validation — proof before trust

Before go-live you prove the migration works: a Proof of Concept (can it work at all?), a Pilot (does it work for real users, in one region?), and testing for performance, data integrity, security, user acceptance (UAT) and cost. Leadership then reviews the results against pre-agreed criteria and makes a GO / CONDITIONAL / NO-GO decision, with a tested rollback plan ready.

Round 3 — facilitating the decision

Part A: Strong answers spot that the design nails reliability & performance (auto-scaling, load balancer) and security (Indian region, encryption, logging), but may under-address cost (auto-scaling can cause bill shock — ask about budgets/alerts) and operations (who monitors it 24×7 in a lean team?).

Part B: There is no official answer — run it as a real board would. The tension: five results are acceptable, but the load test (8× vs a 10× target) fails the very risk FreshFarm exists to fix. Most rigorous teams land on CONDITIONAL GO: fix the 0.02% data mismatch and the medium security issue, and re-run the load test to 10× before launch; a defensible NO-GO until 10× is proven is equally valid. A plain GO ignoring the load gap is the weakest answer — surface why.

Reference

Quick glossary

CapEx / OpEx
A large up-front purchase vs. a flexible pay-as-you-go monthly cost.
IaaS / PaaS / SaaS
Renting servers / renting a ready platform / logging into ready-made software.
Public / Private / Hybrid
Shared provider infrastructure / dedicated to one firm / a mix of both.
Hyperscaler
A giant cloud provider — AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud.
Elasticity / Auto-scaling
Capacity that grows and shrinks automatically with demand.
TCO
Total Cost of Ownership — every cost over a period, not just the sticker price.
ROI / Payback
Return on investment / time to recover the money spent.
The 6 R's
Rehost, Replatform, Repurchase, Refactor, Retire, Retain.
PoC / Pilot
A small throwaway test / a limited real launch to one region or user group.
UAT
User Acceptance Testing — real users confirm real tasks work end-to-end.
Go / No-Go
The evidence-based decision to launch, launch with fixes, or hold.
Rollback
A tested plan to revert to the old system if the launch fails.
Data residency
The rule about which country your data must physically sit in.
DPDP Act
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (2023) governing personal data.
FinOps
Ongoing discipline of watching and optimising the cloud bill.
Saving = on-premise TCO − cloud TCO  ·  ROI = 3-year saving ÷ migration cost  ·  Payback = migration cost ÷ annual saving
