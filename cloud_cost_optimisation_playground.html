Reference

60-second refresher

The ideas you'll use. Skim now; come back during the rounds.

Every saving comes from one of three levers

  • Buy cheaper — lower the rate for the same usage: commitments, spot, licensing, negotiation.
  • Use less — cut the usage and the waste: rightsizing, kill idle, scheduling, storage tiering.
  • Architect smarter — design for efficiency: egress reduction, serverless, Kubernetes efficiency.

…and govern it. A FinOps operating model — tagging, showback, unit economics, forecasting — keeps every saving from creeping back.

The four ways to buy compute

  • On-demand — pay per second, no commitment. Best for unpredictable, spiky load; autoscale it.
  • Commitment (1–3 yr) — up to ~70% off for a steady, always-on baseline. Savings Plans Reservations CUDs
  • Spot — up to ~90% off spare capacity that can be reclaimed. For interruptible, restartable work.
  • Rightsize / kill — the cheapest server is the one you turned off. Do this before you commit.

Sequence with impact vs effort

  • Quick wins — high impact, low effort. Kill idle, rightsize, schedule, first commitments. Do these first.
  • Big bets — high impact, high effort. Modernisation, Kubernetes, negotiation. Plan and staff them.
  • Easy extras — low effort, modest impact. Storage tiering, licensing. Automate and forget.
  • Question it — low impact, high effort. Usually not worth it.

The rule: quick wins fund the engagement; structural work comes next; the operating model makes it stick. Rightsize before you commit — a reservation on an oversized box just locks in the waste.

Round 1 — teaching point

Warm-up on the framework. If a team scores low, they haven't internalised the three levers — send them back to the deck's framework slide before Round 2. The tell: rate changes the price of the same thing (commitments, spot, licences, negotiation); usage changes how much you run (rightsize, kill, schedule, tier); architecture changes the design (CDN, serverless, Kubernetes, managed services). Governance isn't in this round — it's the wrapper that keeps the other three from decaying.

Teaching point: Spot is the distractor here — none of these six findings is a spot case (that's Round 3). The classic slip is answering "rightsize" for everything; each finding has a distinct best fix across three different levers.

The meta-lesson: a consultant's value isn't knowing the tactics — it's sequencing them so early savings fund the hard work, and packaging each as a one-line pitch the CFO will approve.

Glossary

The levers and tactics in play, in one place.

TermWhat it means
Buy cheaper (rate)Lower the unit price for the same usage — commitments, spot, licensing, negotiation.
Use less (usage)Cut consumption and waste — rightsizing, killing idle, scheduling, storage tiering.
Architect smarterDesign for efficiency — egress reduction, serverless, Kubernetes bin-packing.
Commitment1–3 yr discount for steady usage. Savings Plans / RIs Reservations CUDs
Spot / preemptibleDeep discount on spare capacity that can be reclaimed. For interruptible work.
RightsizingShrinking (or killing) resources to match real utilisation. Usually the fastest saving.
Idle / zombieResources running and billed but serving nothing — unattached disks, orphaned snapshots.
SchedulingTurning non-prod off out of hours; autoscaling prod to demand instead of peak 24/7.
Storage tieringLifecycle rules that move cold data to cheaper infrequent-access and archive tiers.
EgressData leaving the cloud or crossing regions. Metered; a CDN cuts it. Inbound is usually free.
FinOpsRunning cloud as a business: Inform → Optimize → Operate, as a continuous loop.
Showback / chargebackShowback shows a team its bill; chargeback makes them pay it. The lever for accountability.
Unit economicsCost per customer / per transaction — the metric that keeps spend honest as you scale.
Impact vs effortThe 2×2 for sequencing: quick wins, big bets, easy extras, and things to question.
